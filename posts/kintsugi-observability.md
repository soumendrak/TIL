Kintsugi (金継ぎ, "golden joinery") is the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with urushi lacquer dusted with powdered gold. The crack is not hidden — it is highlighted with gold. The repair is physically stronger than the original clay, and the unique crack pattern makes the object irreplaceable.

The deeper insight: hiding a crack requires a permanent lie. Displaying it frees the object from pretending. A visibly repaired bowl gets used daily; a "perfect" one gets wrapped and shelved.

## The observability parallel

An AI system fails in production. Two paths:

| Hide the crack | Fill with gold |
|---------------|---------------|
| Patch the symptom, no trace | Capture the full trace |
| No postmortem | Write a postmortem |
| Repeat incident next week | Root cause identified |
| System stays fragile | System is stronger at the repaired point |

This is the structural parallel. Observability is Kintsugi for software — making failures visible, learning from them, and building systems that are more resilient because they've been broken and repaired with care.

The same applies to careers. An unbroken facade creates hidden fragility. A narrative that shows the breaks and the repair has nothing to hide.
